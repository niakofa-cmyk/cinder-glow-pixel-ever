## 2. Is this the application we envisioned?

My honest answer is: **we have evidence of an application structure that could support the vision, but not enough verified implementation evidence to conclude that it has achieved it.**

The distinction is important because there are three different levels of development.

| Level                                            | What it means                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Level 1 — Visual prototype                       | The interface looks like a mixing and mastering application, but important controls may be simulated or incomplete.                                                         |
| Level 2 — Functional audio application           | Users can import music, process real audio, adjust effects, play back results, and export files.                                                                            |
| Level 3 — Intelligent audio engineering platform | The application analyzes recordings, makes context-aware recommendations, validates processing, and produces consistently useful results across different musical material. |

Our vision is Level 3.

A well-designed interface is valuable, and a working audio processor is an essential foundation. But the defining innovation is the intelligence connecting analysis, processing, listening, and verification.

I would not classify Cinder Glow as Level 1, 2, or 3 without examining its implementation and testing its actual behavior.

## 3. The most important areas I want to investigate

Here is the audit I recommend conducting against your current repository.

### A. The actual audio engine

The first question is whether the `engine/` directory implements genuine audio processing.

I want to establish:

* Whether EQ, compression, limiting, and other effects process real audio samples.
* Whether the application supports actual audio-file import and export.
* Whether sample rates and channel configurations are handled correctly.
* Whether audio processing occurs in the browser, on the server, or in a dedicated engine.
* Whether the engine can process a complete song reliably.
* Whether the application preserves the original recording.
* Whether processing controls correspond to real DSP parameters.

**The decisive test:** Import a song, apply a processing change, export the result, and verify that the output reflects the change without introducing unintended artifacts.

### B. The intelligence behind the mixing and mastering

I want to determine whether the application merely applies presets or can actually diagnose audio problems.

For example, can it distinguish between a vocal that needs more level and one that is being masked by guitars?

Can it identify excessive bass energy without automatically removing musically important low frequencies?

Can it recognize when a recording already sounds good and recommend leaving it alone?

Can it compare alternative processing chains at matched loudness?

These are the capabilities that would move the application beyond conventional preset-driven mastering.

### C. The AI architecture

I want to inspect how the application makes its decisions.

Specifically:

* Which AI models or algorithms are used?
* What information is passed to them?
* Are their recommendations grounded in actual audio measurements?
* Can recommendations be tested before being applied?
* Are processing changes reversible?
* Does the application measure the result after processing?
* Can it recognize when a proposed change makes the audio worse?

The ideal architecture uses deterministic DSP for signal processing and carefully validated AI models for tasks such as classification, diagnosis, recommendation, and workflow automation.

The AI should not have to guess whether the audio clipped when a reliable measurement can answer the question directly.

### D. Professional mastering quality

I want to verify whether the application has a complete, trustworthy mastering workflow.

That includes:

* Integrated, short-term, and momentary loudness measurement.
* True-peak analysis.
* Tonal balance assessment.
* Stereo correlation and mono compatibility.
* Reference-track comparison.
* Loudness-matched A/B playback.
* Export validation.
* Before-and-after measurement reports.

A professional-looking loudness meter is not sufficient. Its calculations must be correct, and its readings must correspond to the audio being processed.

### E. Engineering quality and reliability

Finally, I want to inspect the application's build process, testing, dependencies, and runtime behavior.

The presence of `.github/workflows/` is a useful starting point, but we need to establish whether those workflows actually run meaningful tests.

I would examine:

* TypeScript and application architecture.
* DSP implementation and numerical correctness.
* Test coverage.
* Dependency security.
* Audio-processing performance.
* Error handling.
* Project persistence.
* File-upload and export reliability.
* Browser compatibility.
* Server resource consumption.
* Deployment configuration.

This will help us identify which problems should be fixed before adding more features.

## 4. How I would make Cinder Glow substantially better

Regardless of what the detailed audit reveals, I recommend organizing the product around six interconnected systems.

**System 1: Audio Foundation**

A reliable processing engine, file handling, transport, routing, gain management, and high-quality export.

**System 2: Sonic Analysis**

Frequency analysis, loudness, dynamics, stereo behavior, transient characteristics, and audio-quality diagnostics.

**System 3: Mixing Intelligence**

Track analysis, frequency masking, source relationships, contextual recommendations, and optional automated mixing.

**System 4: Mastering Intelligence**

Reference comparisons, adaptive processing, loudness management, final quality control, and export verification.

**System 5: Sonic Intelligence Assistant**

A system that explains problems in plain language, recommends possible solutions, generates alternatives, and validates the results.

**System 6: Quality Assurance Laboratory**

Automated DSP tests, performance benchmarks, regression testing, reference audio, and controlled listening comparisons.

These should be integrated into a coherent product rather than developed as disconnected collections of features.

### My proposed signature feature: Sonic Intelligence

I would make this the defining experience of Cinder Glow.

Imagine importing a song and receiving an analysis like this:

**Vocal clarity: Needs attention in the chorus**

The system identifies that electric guitars and backing vocals compete with the lead vocal during several sections.

It presents three alternative solutions:

1. Adjust the vocal level during the affected phrases.
2. Apply dynamic EQ to the competing guitar frequencies.
3. Reduce competing reverberation during the densest sections.

You audition each option against the original, with playback levels matched closely enough to support a fair comparison.

The software then measures the changes, reports any tradeoffs, and lets you choose which result best serves the music.

That is the experience I would aim to make exceptional.

The system must not claim that a mathematical measurement proves a mix sounds better. It should combine objective evidence with listening and user preference.
